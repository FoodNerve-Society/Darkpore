# Hero Section Redesign: Category-First Visualization (Light Mode)

Since an algorithm to pick the "Top 7" isn't feasible yet, the smartest approach is to make **Categorization** the primary interaction of the Hero Section. 

The challenge: How do we make picking a category *fun* and *dynamic* without just dumping a boring grid of 10 category cards on the screen? 

Here are three highly engaging, uncluttered ways to achieve this in Light Mode:

---

## 1. The "Dynamic Accordion" (The Tactile Approach)
* **The Concept:** The entire hero section is divided into 4 or 5 massive, full-width horizontal (or vertical) bands. Each band is just the name of a Category in huge, elegant typography (e.g., "Food Security", "Energy").
* **The Interaction:** When a user clicks a band, it smoothly and dramatically expands, while the other bands shrink away. Inside the expanded band, a beautiful, horizontal slider of articles for *just that category* fades in. 
* **Why it works:** It is completely devoid of clutter. At first glance, there are literally zero article cards on the screen—just big, beautiful words. It feels incredibly tactile and fun to open and close the bands, appealing to younger users, while the massive click targets are perfect for older users.

## 2. The "Interactive Wheel / Sticky List" (The Preview Approach)
* **The Concept:** The screen is split in half. The left side features a massive, bold list of categories (or a visual "dial"). 
* **The Interaction:** As the user clicks or scrolls down the list of categories on the left, the right side of the screen instantly reacts. If they click "Policy," the right side dynamically fans out a stack of cards showing the latest Policy articles. 
* **Why it works:** It acts like a real-time filter. The user never leaves the hero section, but they get to "play" with the list on the left to see what pops up on the right. It feels like a high-end dashboard.

## 3. The "Mad Libs" Explorer (The Conversational Approach)
* **The Concept:** Instead of traditional UI, the hero features a massive, bold, fill-in-the-blank sentence in the center of the screen.
  * Example: *"I want to explore the latest intelligence regarding [ **Food Security** ⌄ ] specifically focusing on [ **Startups** ⌄ ]."*
* **The Interaction:** The bracketed words are giant, interactive dropdowns. When the user changes a word, 3 beautiful article cards instantly slide up from the bottom of the screen matching their exact criteria.
* **Why it works:** It gamifies the discovery process. It doesn't feel like navigating a website; it feels like having a conversation with an intelligence engine. It is highly engaging for young users and explicitly clear for older users because it is literally written in plain English.
